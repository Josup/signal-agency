# The Six Google Business Profile Fields That Change Which Queries You're Eligible For

Your Google Business Profile doesn't just populate your Knowledge Panel. It controls which prompts your business is eligible to answer. Miss a field or fill it incorrectly, and AI search engines won't retrieve you for entire categories of intent — even if your site has perfect content and markup.

We build every client site with this in mind. The structure matters as much as the schema.

## The Business Category Field: Your Primary Retrieval Gate

The category you list in GBP is not decorative. It's your primary eligibility filter.

When someone prompts "best optometrist in Crown Heights" or "vision care near me," AI engines check your GBP category against their intent parser. If you're listed as "Optometrist" instead of "Eye Care Center," you might get retrieved. If you're "General Services," you won't.

Nostrand Optical is listed as both Optometrist and Eye Care Center. That dual listing makes them eligible for prompts around vision exams, contact lenses, eyewear selection, and diabetic eye screening — categories that a single listing would miss.

The rule: use your most specific, industry-standard category. Not "Business Services." Not "Professional Services." The exact thing you do.

Check your category against what your customers actually search for. If they say "I need an eye doctor," your category should match that language, not internal taxonomy.

## The Service Area Field: Borough Versus Neighborhood Resolution

A service area business — one without a storefront where customers walk in — needs the Service Area field filled correctly. This field tells AI engines where you operate and how far you'll travel.

If you fill Service Area as "Brooklyn," engines treat you as answering all Brooklyn queries. If you fill it as "Crown Heights, Williamsburg," you're eligible for neighborhood-specific prompts but not citywide ones.

This is the inverse of what most people think. Broader is not better. Narrower is often more retrievable because you're competing against fewer businesses in your stated service area.

If you serve Crown Heights only, say so. An AI engine will cite a Crown Heights-focused business for a Crown Heights prompt before it cites a business that claims all of Brooklyn.

Brooklyn BJJ Lessons lists Crown Heights as their service area. A prompt for "BJJ classes in Crown Heights" retrieves them before gyms claiming all of Brooklyn.

## The Phone Number Field: The Verification Signal

Your phone number in GBP has to match your phone number on your site, in your schema markup, and in citation directories. This consistency signals to AI engines that you're a real, verifiable business.

If your GBP lists 718-555-0100 but your site markup lists 718-555-0101, engines flag the mismatch. Some engines weight verified, consistent numbers higher in retrieval. Others exclude mismatched ones entirely.

Verify your number with Google, then hardcode it everywhere. Check it quarterly. A simple copy error can cost you retrieval eligibility across platforms.

## The Hours Field: The Freshness Gate

Your business hours appear in AI responses. More importantly, they're a freshness signal.

Engines check whether your posted hours match when you're actually open. If you list 9am–6pm but your content is dated and your last citation came three months ago, engines infer you might be closed or outdated. A business with current hours and recent content gets retrieved more reliably.

Update your hours the moment they change — even if it's just seasonal. Seasonal hours especially signal to an engine that you're active and maintaining your profile.

Brooklyn BJJ Lessons keeps their hours current and their GBP refreshed weekly. That consistency feeds into retrieval.

## The Website Field: The Authority Link

This field must match your canonical domain exactly.

If your GBP lists example.com but your site lives at www.example.com, or if you point to a landing page instead of your homepage, you're fragmenting your authority signal. Engines see two domains; they're unsure which one to cite.

Point directly to your homepage. Keep it consistent across GBP, schema, and directories. This single field is often overlooked and costs retrieval eligibility.

## The Description Field: The Snippet Feeder

Your 750-character business description in GBP is fair game for AI engines to pull verbatim.

Write it as retrieval-grade content, not marketing copy. Include your specific services, your neighborhood, your credentials if relevant. Use plain language. Make it quotable.

If your description says "we offer optometry services," an engine might cite you. If it says "comprehensive eye exams, contact lens fittings, diabetic retinopathy screening, and eyewear selection in Crown Heights," you're eligible for each of those specific services in prompts.

## Audit Your Profile in Ten Minutes

Open your GBP. Open your site schema in a browser inspector. Open one citation directory (Yelp, Apple Maps, or your industry directory). Cross-check these six fields:

1. Category — is it specific and searchable?
2. Service Area — does it match your actual operating radius?
3. Phone — does it match your site markup exactly?
4. Hours — are they current and refreshed regularly?
5. Website — does it point to your canonical homepage?
6. Description — is it specific enough that an engine would quote it?

If any field is blank, inconsistent, or generic, fill it. Consistency across GBP, your site, and your directories is what makes retrieval possible.

The engines don't care about polish. They care about clarity and consistency. Six fields. Ten minutes. The difference between invisibility and being cited by name in a ChatGPT response.
